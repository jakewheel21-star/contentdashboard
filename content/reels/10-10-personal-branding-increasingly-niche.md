# Reel format: "10/10 personal branding advice, but it gets increasingly more niche"

Every point comes from the Brand-building resources library on Drive (Personal Brand Blueprint, Personal branding on video, Organic content strategy, Hooks, Storytelling, Coach's Script Playbook, Carousels & pinned posts, Video editing for retention, 24 Edit Moves, Colour Grading in CapCut).

---

## The format

| Beat | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0–1.5s | Say the hook line straight to camera. On-screen text: **10/10 BRAND ADVICE** with a small "↓ more niche" arrow. Start talking on frame one: no "hey guys". |
| Point 1 | ~5–7s | Level 1 (broad). Make the point, then give the reason in one line. |
| Point 2 | ~5–7s | Level 2. Punch in 10–20% to show it's moved up a level. |
| Point 3 | ~5–7s | Level 3. Change the on-screen counter (e.g. "NICHE LEVEL 3"). |
| Point 4 | ~5–7s | Level 4. Slow down slightly, because the specifics are the payoff. |
| Point 5 | ~6–8s | Level 5 (ultra niche). The "wait, what?" point. Hold a beat before you say it. |
| CTA | ~3s | One ask only. Cut on the last word with no outro. |

**Total:** 35–45s, roughly 110–125 spoken words.

**The rule that makes it work:** each point has to be more specific than the one before. Level 1 is something anyone could say. By Level 5 it should be a setting, a number or a slider that only someone who does the work would know. The payoff lands at the end, so people stay for the most niche one.

**Assembly:** take one point from each level (1→5). Picking points from the same theme across levels (e.g. all about "trust", or all about "the edit") makes the video feel deliberate. Mixing themes gives it more variety. Both work.

**Edit notes:** use a niche-level counter or progress bar in the top 40% of the frame, with captions in the 48–66% band. Play the same soft "pop" sound every time the level changes. Use a riser and hit before Level 5 only.

## The point bank

Each point has two parts: **the line** (what you say) and **→ the why** (one line of reason). Say both.

### LEVEL 1: Broad (anyone could say it, but it's true)

1. **Decide what you want from your brand before you post another video.** → Posting consistently with no destination can take you further away from it.
2. **You already have a personal brand.** → If you don't define it, your audience defines it for you, and you might not like the answer.
3. **Optimise for trust, not reach.** → A small audience of the right people out-earns a big audience of the wrong ones.
4. **People buy from people.** → A face, a voice and an opinion earn more trust than any logo.
5. **Tell the story, not the adjective.** → "Hard-working" is forgettable. The night you rebuilt a client's launch at 2am isn't.
6. **Give way more than you ask.** → Selling in every post reads as desperate.
7. **Repeat your message until you're bored of it, then keep going.** → You'll be sick of it long before your audience has heard it.
8. **Real beats polished.** → With AI content everywhere, a flawless aesthetic doesn't stand out any more.
9. **Stop chasing virality.** → It changes what you make and who you attract.
10. **Be the expert or the student, and say which.** → A student pretending to be an expert gets found out.
11. **Show your losses, not just your wins.** → A feed with only wins looks like a performance.
12. **"I'm not a camera person" is a practice problem, not a personality trait.** → Nobody starts out natural on camera.

### LEVEL 2: Strategy (more specific, still big-picture)

1. **Do the buyer maths.** → Monthly target divided by your price. If it's eight, you don't need 100k views. You need eight people to trust you.
2. **Write a "won't be linked with" list.** → A bad association costs you more than a good one earns. Most people skip this list.
3. **Before any collab or trend, ask one question:** if this became the most-seen thing I ever made, would I be glad? → If not, say no.
4. **One topic per account, one viewer per video.** → If you post about everything, the algorithm can't work out who to show you to.
5. **Plan your content by job, not format: reach, trust, ask.** → Most people skip trust content. It gets the fewest views and does the most work.
6. **Only ask for the sale about twice a month.** → Give each ask a reason why now: spaces open, a cohort date, a new service.
7. **Write down ten things your industry says that you disagree with.** → Circle the three you'd defend in front of a room. Those are your best hooks.
8. **Name your method.** → If it has a name, people can talk about it and recommend it.
9. **Help people interpret their world, not just follow steps.** → How-to content is everywhere. A new way of seeing the problem is what people follow.
10. **Don't start your ideas from competitors' best posts.** → You'll attract more people like them, not more clients.
11. **Pick one hero platform plus email, and commit for six months.** → Being on five platforms half-heartedly is how you go nowhere.
12. **Name a series.** → "Client question #12" tells people what they get if they follow, and you don't have to decide what to post every day.
13. **Check your monthly stats in this order:** enquiries, then saves, sends and comments that quote you. Views come last. → Slow growth with the right people means it's working.
14. **Coin shared language for your audience.** → Name the stages and stuck points they go through. People who use your words feel like they're part of something.
15. **Sprint, then maintain.** → Grow in 30–90 day pushes, then hold at one or two posts a week. Weeks with zero posts cost you.

### LEVEL 3: Content craft (the stuff you do every week)

1. **Open with their problem, in their words, not your credentials.** → "You've been posting for months and nobody's watching" beats "I'm a strategist with ten years' experience."
2. **Steal your hooks from your DMs.** → The exact phrases clients use, like "200-view jail", beat polished copy every time.
3. **Every video should build one of four things:** likability, admirability, credibility or trustability. → If it builds none, don't post it.
4. **Write "how I", not "how to".** → People hear "how to" all day. A personal result is easier to trust.
5. **Pin three posts: who you help, proof, and your story or method.** → New visitors decide whether to follow in seconds, and those three do the talking.
6. **Your bio needs one line only you could write.** → "I help X do Y" is clear but it isn't memorable. Keep the clarity and add the personality.
7. **Mine your sales calls for doubts.** → Answer one objection per video, before they've had a chance to raise it.
8. **Swap every "and then" in your script for "but" or "so".** → "And then" is a list. "But" and "so" make it a story.
9. **Zoom into one moment.** → "It was a hard year" is a summary. "Tuesday, 6am, sat in the van, thinking I can't pay Friday's wages" is a story.
10. **State the stakes in the first 20 seconds.** → No stakes means nothing is at risk, so nothing matters.
11. **Write the last line first.** → In short-form, make it lead back into the first line so the video loops.
12. **Rule out the obvious answer early.** → "It's not that you need more willpower" makes them lean in.
13. **One idea a week becomes ten pieces of content:** one long piece, one carousel, three shorts and a few stories. → Most people never see everything, so repurposing isn't repeating yourself.
14. **Film three shorts from the same idea with three hook types:** a warning, a positive and a story. → Same idea, three chances to land.
15. **If you use AI on a script, ask it what to cut, not what to write.** → If it writes the script, you'll sound like everyone else.
16. **Mark your two asks in the calendar first.** → Then fill the days before each one with trust content that sets it up.

### LEVEL 4: Filming and editing tactics (now we're getting specific)

1. **Your hook needs to land by 1.5 seconds, not 3.** → The median scroll-stop on TikTok is 1.9 seconds, and UK viewers are even faster.
2. **Interrupt first, then add meaning.** → Frame one breaks the feed's rhythm with motion, an angle, a prop or a sound. The words come second.
3. **Pause at 0.5 seconds with the sound off.** → If you can't tell what the video's about, neither can the viewer.
4. **Put your payoff in the back half.** → If the answer arrives in the first 15 seconds, the rest is dead air.
5. **Delete every line before your first real claim.** → "Hi, so today I want to talk about" goes. Every time.
6. **Don't cut every pause.** → Keep the breath before your big line and the odd "um". If you sand every take smooth, you sound synthetic.
7. **Something should change on screen every five seconds or so.** → If eight seconds pass with nothing new, add a punch-in, a cutaway or a keyword.
8. **Only punch in on the lines that matter.** → If you punch in on every sentence, it stops meaning anything.
9. **B-roll should extend what you're saying, not repeat it.** → Saying "phone" and showing a phone adds nothing. Show what happens next.
10. **No outro.** → Cut on the last word of the payoff. "Thanks for watching" is where people swipe.
11. **Build your own sound kit: three or four sounds, in every video.** → People will start recognising your videos by ear.
12. **Use a riser and hit once, before your single biggest moment.** → You only get to cry wolf once.
13. **Choose two signature elements that are in every frame.** → A colour, a mic, glasses. Start from what you already wear so it never feels like a costume.
14. **Change your set if it looks like everyone else's.** → If every competitor has the same shelf and coloured lights, you blend in.
15. **Batch the first two weeks before day one of a sprint.** → Starting a sprint with nothing banked is how you burn out by week three.
16. **Carousels: slides two and three each have to work as a hook on their own.** → Instagram can show someone slide two on a later visit, so it needs to stop people too.

### LEVEL 5: Ultra niche (the "how do you even know that" tier)

1. **Lock your exposure on your phone, then drag it down a touch.** → Press and hold on your face, then slide brightness down. A dark shot can be lifted, but a blown-out one is gone for good.
2. **Never mix daylight with household bulbs.** → You get one orange cheek and one blue cheek, and no slider can fix both.
3. **Grade with your screen at 70% brightness.** → If your screen's turned up, your video looks too dark on everyone else's phone.
4. **Correct first, grade second.** → A stylish filter on uncorrected footage just gives you a stylish version of the problem.
5. **Put every filter at 30–40%, never 100.** → At full strength it looks like a filter. At 30% it looks like good footage.
6. **Turn on the scopes in CapCut.** → Your eyes get used to an orange picture within seconds. The scopes don't.
7. **Healthy skin sits on one line on the vectorscope, whatever your skin tone.** → If your skin leans green, add magenta. If it leans red, add green.
8. **Make your brand colour pop with HSL.** → Pick it with the picker, add 10–20 saturation and leave everything else alone.
9. **Fake a face light in CapCut.** → Duplicate the clip, add +5 to +12 exposure on the copy, then circle-mask it over your face and feather it heavily.
10. **Lower the saturation of greens, yellows and blues slightly.** → Your skin becomes the most colourful thing in the frame, and it instantly looks more premium.
11. **Save your grade as a preset named after where you film.** → "Studio corner, daylight". Use it on everything for three months, because a look becomes a brand through repetition.
12. **Don't push sliders past about 15.** → On well-lit phone footage, most settings land between −15 and +15. If you're at 50, something's wrong.
13. **Keep text out of the top, right edge and bottom of the frame.** → That's where the buttons and captions sit. Graphics go in the top 40%, captions about halfway down.
14. **Captions: one to six words per screen, and one emphasis style for the whole video.** → Three colours and two fonts in your captions looks cheap.
15. **If a sound effect repeats close together, shift the pitch slightly each time.** → The same pop at the same pitch on every cut sounds robotic.
16. **Lower the mid frequencies of your music under your voice, not just the volume.** → That's where speech sits. Then every word is clear on a phone speaker.
17. **Cut the music dead before the line that matters.** → The silence puts a spotlight on the next sentence.
18. **Carousel covers: three colours, two fonts and one layout, fixed for good.** → A fourth strong colour is where covers start looking messy.
19. **Shrink your carousel cover to thumbnail size before posting.** → If you can't read it there, nobody's swiping.
20. **Add music to carousels.** → It's widely reported to push them into Explore, and it costs nothing.
21. **Put your search phrase in the bold name line of your profile, not just the bio.** → It's what the platform reads first when matching you to searches.
22. **Personal posts with a link get about 27% fewer impressions.** → Put the link in the comments or your profile instead.
23. **Wear mid-tones on camera, not pure white or pure black.** → White blows out and black turns into a black hole.
24. **Turn HDR off when you film talking heads.** → It often exports washed out or far too bright on other people's phones.

## Quick check before filming each one

- [ ] The first word is the hook. No warm-up.
- [ ] Level 5 is clearly more niche than Level 1. If you could swap them round, rework it.
- [ ] Every point has a "why", not just the instruction.
- [ ] There's only one CTA, and it relates to the topic.
- [ ] It runs to 45 seconds or less (about 125 words max).
- [ ] You'd be glad if this were the video you're known for.
